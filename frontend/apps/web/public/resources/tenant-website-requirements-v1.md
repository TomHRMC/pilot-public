## 1\. Why You Need a Website for 10DLC

When you send text messages to your customers through Pilot for Trades, those messages travel over the US carriers' **10DLC** (10-digit long code) business-messaging network. Every business that texts customers must register a **brand** and **campaign** with **The Campaign Registry (TCR),** the organization the carriers use to vet who is sending business SMS and why. TCR does not take your word for it. As part of brand vetting, TCR verifies that your business has a **real, public website** that proves you are a legitimate operation. This catches many sole proprietors by surprise: **Registrations without a website, or with a website that is missing the required pages, are likely to be rejected.**

### 1.1 What TCR verifies

| \#  | Requirement                                                  | What the vetter looks for                                                                                                  |
| :-: | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|  1  | **A website URL** on your brand registration                 | A working public URL that loads your business's site                                                                       |
|  2  | **About, Contact, Terms & Conditions, Privacy Policy pages** | Each page reachable from your site, no login required                                                                      |
|  3  | **SMS-specific disclosure language in your Privacy Policy**  | Message types, texting cadence, rate notices, opt-out instructions, and a no-third-party-sharing statement (see §3)        |
|  4  | **Terms & Privacy URLs in your messaging campaign**          | The exact URLs of these two pages, submitted with your campaign's call-to-action (Pilot fills these in from your settings) |

A missing or incomplete website is one of the most common causes of 10DLC rejection, and a rejection means your customer texts (appt confirmations, reminders, invoices) cannot be sent until it is fixed and re-vetted.

### 1.2 Your two options with Pilot

- **Host your own site.** If you already have a website — or prefer to build one — this guide tells you exactly what it must contain. Work through §2–§4, then check every box in §6 before you submit (or re-submit) your 10DLC registration.
- **Let Pilot host it.** If you don't have a website, Pilot can publish a compliant 3-page site for your business (landing, Privacy Policy, Terms of Service) at your own web address, free on all paid plans. Everything in this guide is handled for you automatically. You'll find this under **Settings → Website** in your Pilot dashboard.

Either way, the requirements below are the same — they come from TCR and the carriers, not from us.

## 2\. Required Pages

Your site needs **three pages**, all publicly accessible. A bare domain with only legal pages — or only a homepage — underperforms in vetting.

### 2.1 Landing page (your homepage)

This is the business-identity page. It must show, without scrolling through anything unusual:

- **Your business name** — exactly as registered (or your DBA), prominent at the top.
- **What you do** — a sentence or two of plain description ("Residential and commercial electrical services in Richmond, VA"). This is your "About" evidence.
- **How to reach you** — a phone number, an email address, or both. This is your "Contact" evidence.
- **Your city and state.**
- **Visible links to your Privacy Policy and Terms of Service** — typically in the footer.

Your street address is **not** required. If you work from home, show city and state only — do not publish your home address just for vetting.

### 2.2 Privacy Policy page

A real privacy policy at a public URL (e.g. `yourbusiness.com/privacy`). Beyond the standard privacy content, it **must** contain the SMS-specific clauses in §3. Their absence is a major source of 10DLC rejection.

### 2.3 Terms of Service page

Your terms of service at a public URL (e.g. `yourbusiness.com/terms`), including the SMS program terms in §4.

### 2.4 Accessibility rules (all three pages)

- **Public** — no login, no password, no "request access."
- **Directly loadable** — the vetter pastes your URL into a browser; it must load as-is.
- **Mobile-loadable** — the pages must open and be readable on a phone.
- **No placeholders** — "Coming soon," lorem ipsum, template-demo content, or parked-domain pages are vetting failures.
- **Reachable legal links** — the Privacy and Terms links must be visible on the landing page (header or footer).

## 3\. Privacy Policy SMS Clauses

Your Privacy Policy must include a section covering your text-messaging program. TCR looks for **six specific disclosures**:

| \#  | Clause                            | What it must say                                                                                                       |
| :-: | :-------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|  1  | **Message types**                 | What kinds of texts you send (appointment confirmations, service updates, reminders, account alerts, review requests…) |
|  2  | **Message frequency / cadence**   | How often customers can expect texts ("typically no more than N per month")                                            |
|  3  | **Rates notice**                  | "Message and data rates may apply."                                                                                    |
|  4  | **Opt-out instructions**          | How to stop texts — reply **STOP**                                                                                     |
|  5  | **Help instructions**             | How to get help — reply **HELP** (or contact you directly)                                                             |
|  6  | **No third-party mobile sharing** | A statement that you do not share mobile information with third parties for marketing or promotional purposes          |

###

### 3.1 Ready-to-adapt sample (Privacy Policy — "SMS Communications" section)

> **SMS Communications**  
> We may send you text messages regarding appointment confirmations, service updates, and account alerts. Message frequency varies but is typically no more than 5 messages per month.
>
> Message and data rates may apply.
>
> To opt out, reply **STOP** to any message. For help, reply **HELP**.
>
> We do not share mobile information with third parties for marketing or promotional purposes.

**Adapt — don't copy blindly.** Change the message types to match what you actually send through Pilot, and state a cadence you genuinely honor. Vetting cross-checks your stated program against your campaign registration; a mismatch is its own rejection risk.

## 4\. Terms of Service SMS Clauses

Your Terms of Service must describe the SMS program itself, what the customer is agreeing to when they give you their mobile number. Include a "Text Messaging" (or "SMS Program") section covering:

| \#  | Clause                  | What it must say                                                                                                                                      |
| :-: | :---------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|  1  | **Program description** | The name/nature of the program ("Service notifications from {Your Business}") and the message types                                                   |
|  2  | **Consent / opt-in**    | That customers provide express consent to receive texts (e.g. when booking, or by requesting texts) — and that consent is not a condition of purchase |
|  3  | **Opt-out**             | Reply **STOP** at any time; you will honor opt-outs promptly                                                                                          |
|  4  | **Help**                | Reply **HELP**, or contact your support email/phone                                                                                                   |
|  5  | **Rates notice**        | "Message and data rates may apply."                                                                                                                   |
|  6  | **Frequency**           | The same cadence stated in your Privacy Policy                                                                                                        |

###

### 4.1 Ready-to-adapt sample (Terms of Service — "Text Messaging" section)

> **Text Messaging**  
> When you provide your mobile number and ask to receive text messages from {Your Business}, you expressly consent to receive service-related texts, such as appointment confirmations, scheduling updates, and account alerts, from us. Consent to receive texts is not a condition of purchasing our services.
>
> Message frequency varies but is typically no more than 5 messages per month. Message and data rates may apply.
>
> To opt out, reply **STOP** to any message. For help, reply **HELP**.

### 4.2 Where the URLs go

When Pilot submits your 10DLC campaign, the registration includes the **Terms & Conditions URL** and **Privacy Policy URL** under the campaign's call-to-action. Keep these two pages at stable URLs — if you move them, tell us (or update your website URL in **Settings → Messaging**) before the old links go stale, because re-vetting checks the URLs as submitted.

## 5\. Worked Example: A Compliant Small-Business Site

### Figure 1: Example compliant small-business landing page content

| ABC Electric Residential & commercial · Richmond, VA · (804) 555-0142 · name@abc.example.com About this business ABC Electric has been serving Richmond since 2015\. We handle residential service calls, panel upgrades, and small commercial fit-outs.. Privacy Policy · Terms of Service |
| :-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------: |

Why this passes:

- **Business name** is the most prominent element — matches the registered brand name.
- **Contact information above the fold** — phone and email visible without scrolling.
- **City and state** shown; no home street address needed.
- **"About" section** — two plain sentences establish what the business does.
- **Legal page links** — Privacy Policy and Terms of Service linked directly on the page.

### Figure 2: Required SMS clauses inside the Privacy Policy

#### SMS Communications

We may send you text messages regarding appointment confirmations, service updates, and account alerts.  
Message frequency varies but is typically no more than 5 messages per month.  
Message and data rates may apply.  
To opt out, reply STOP to any message. For help, reply HELP.  
We do not share mobile information with third parties for marketing or promotional purposes.

Annotations:

- **Message types** — what texts the customer will receive.
- **Frequency / cadence** — an explicit, honest number.
- **Rates notice** — the exact "message and data rates may apply" statement.
- **STOP opt-out** and **HELP instructions** — both keywords present.
- **No third-party mobile sharing** — the clause vetters specifically scan for.

## 6\. Compliance Checklist

Before you submit (or re-submit) your 10DLC registration, verify every box. If **Pilot hosts your site** (Settings → Website), this checklist is already satisfied by your published pages.

☐ Landing page with business name visible  
☐ Phone number or email address visible without scrolling  
☐ City and state visible  
☐ "About this business" section with descriptive text  
☐ Privacy Policy page with all six SMS disclosure clauses (§3)  
☐ Terms of Service page with the SMS program terms (§4)  
☐ Privacy Policy link on the landing page  
☐ Terms of Service link on the landing page  
☐ All pages accessible at a public URL (no login, no placeholder content)  
☐ Pages load on mobile devices

### After a rejection

If your brand registration was **rejected for website content**: fix the gaps above, then re-submit from **Settings → Messaging** in your Pilot dashboard. If you switch to a Pilot-hosted site after a rejection, publish the site first, confirm the website-address update when prompted, and then re-submit — TCR checks the URL exactly as it was submitted to the carrier, so the new address must be in place **before** you re-apply.
